What is IT Story? Read the history of computers, the internet, OS, web and databases as timelines

ArcherLab IT Story is a free learning site that follows IT history from the 1830s telegraph through computers, operating systems, the web and servers, and databases, as year-by-year timelines. Each entry starts with the problem and the people behind a technology, and unfamiliar terms come with short explanations. It runs in the browser with no signup or install.

Who it is for

  • Developers and CS students who want to know why technology looks the way it does
  • Beginners using the web, servers or databases who want the background
  • General readers interested in the history of IT

How to use it

  1. From the home page, choose a topic: computers, networks, operating systems, web, web servers or databases.
  2. Select a year card on the timeline to open what happened that year and why.
  3. The web making timeline splits into four parts — structure, styling, interaction and tools — so read them in turn.
  4. If you are unsure where to begin, start with the study-order guide (Korean).

Features

  • Topic timelines: computers, networks, operating systems, databases, web making and web servers
  • Year cards that explain the problem of the time and its effect on later technology
  • Short explanations for unfamiliar terms
  • Korean and English pages

Good to know

Dates and interpretations differ between sources. If you need to cite IT history formally, check the original sources too.

The security and protocol/standards timelines are not available yet.

Frequently asked questions

Is IT Story free?

Yes. It is free and needs no account.

What topics does it cover?

Computers, networks, operating systems, databases, web making and web servers. Security and protocols/standards are in progress.

Where should I start?

Pick any topic that interests you. The Korean study-order guide suggests a path from computers to the web.

What is a year card?

A card on the timeline that opens a short explanation of what happened that year and its background.

Is there a Korean version?

Yes. The Korean home page is the main version and each timeline has both languages.

Are the internet and the web the same?

No. The internet is the network infrastructure and the web is a service that runs on it. The Korean "Internet vs Web" guide walks through one request.
